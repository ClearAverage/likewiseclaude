  1. 01

    Roll up your class

    One student hosts. Name the class, pick the date results drop, and share the 6‑letter code. The date is locked in. Nobody can move it.

  2. 02

    Everyone joins

    Classmates sign in and enter the code. The host checks every name is someone real, removes anyone sketchy, and hits Start. Doors close.

  3. 03

    Pick in secret

    Choose one person. Change your mind as often as you like until reveal day. Your pick is sealed, and nobody can see it. Not even the host.

  4. 04

    Reveal day

    If your crush picked you too, you both see it’s a match. If not, you’ll see “No mutual match this round.” They never find out. Three days later, the class disappears.

Privacy, for real

Built so nobody
can peek.

Not your classmates. Not the host. Not the kid in the back row with DevTools open. Picks are sealed by the database’s own security rules, so a pick can only ever be read when it’s mutual.

Sealed picks

Your pick is locked by server-side rules. No app, no account, no script can read it unless your crush picked you back.

Hosts see nothing

Hosting gives you a Start button, not a backstage pass. The host can’t see picks, can’t move the reveal date, and can’t end it early.

Frozen at reveal

One pick per person, and it freezes the second results drop. Nobody can switch picks afterwards to “test” who liked them.

Gone in 3 days

Results stay up for three days, then the class locks for good and gets wiped. No permanent record of who liked who.

Real people only

Every account verifies its email, and names are locked once a class starts. The host approves the roster before anyone can pick.

One-tap reports

Fake name? Something unkind? Report it from inside the class and it goes straight to the moderators.
